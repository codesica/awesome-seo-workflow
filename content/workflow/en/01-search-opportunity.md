# Need and search opportunity

Identify a real need before choosing a topic.

## Entry requirement

A website direction is enough; existing traffic and paid keyword tools are not required.

## Goal

Choose one evidenced question you can credibly answer on a useful page.

## Concepts first

- Query: words entered into search. Intent: the task behind them; a query can have several intents.

- Evidence of need, search volume, and commercial value are different. One real question does not establish monthly demand or willingness to pay.

## Inputs

- One sentence describing your audience and the experience, material, or product you can offer.

- Existing sites can bring a recent Search Console query export; new sites can start with interviews, support questions, or public discussions.

## Actions

### 01.1 Collect real questions

Collect 5–10 questions from accessible customer conversations, public communities, or your own practice notes. Record wording, source or traceable reference, date, audience, and context. Remove personal identifiers. Mark unsupported ideas as hypotheses.

Output: A question list separating observations from hypotheses.

### 01.2 Inspect search results

Search three candidate questions in the target language and region. Record the date, query, and the formats of several organic results: guide, tool, product, comparison, or local service. Open the pages to inspect their answers. Separate ads from organic results. Treat this as a small observation, not a comprehensive ranking study.

Output: An intent hypothesis, page format, and unmet question for each candidate.

### 01.3 Choose one feasible question

For each candidate ask: Does someone actually face this? Can I provide useful steps, examples, or evidence? Can one page deliver it? Does it fit my site? Mark yes/no/unknown and choose the clearest feasible opportunity rather than inventing a precision score.

Output: One primary question, rationale, and a deferred list.

### 01.4 Record data limits

If using Google Trends, record region, period, and relative interest; it is not an absolute query count. Keep provider, geography, period, and date for third-party estimates. Without reliable data, leave volume unknown, not zero, and never ask AI to fabricate it.

Output: An opportunity card containing evidence and unknowns.

## Tools

- A browser and notes or spreadsheet suffice. Google Trends is optional; Search Console needs property access and available data.

## Deliverables

- Opportunity card fields: audience, context, primary question, intent, sources, dates, region/language, distinctive help, volume with provenance or unknown, and next step.

## Acceptance criteria

- At least one traceable need signal, a specific question, and a reason for your page to exist. Unsupported ideas can remain exploratory but cannot be labeled validated opportunities.

## Stop conditions

- No evidenced need or credible ability to answer: research further or find a qualified reviewer before producing pages. Stop on sensitive specialist topics beyond your competence.

## Next step

Take the opportunity card to stage 2 and design one page.
