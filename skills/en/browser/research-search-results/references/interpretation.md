# Research Criteria
Intent labels may be informational / commercial-investigation / transactional / navigational / local / mixed, but must be supported by results and user tasks, not just literal keyword wording. Label explanations of "why the user searches for this" as inference.

The comparison matrix should examine target audience, how the task is completed, coverage, original evidence, verifiable sources, basis for update dates, readability, and interaction friction. If data/author/update time is not shown, that only means it was not observed; it does not mean the page has no such information.

Google results are influenced by language, region, device, and other context. Disabling personalization does not remove factors such as region. A single browser observation cannot represent all users or ranking history. Lists returned by search tools/APIs must not be presented as the browser's current screen.

site: can help discover page samples, but does not guarantee all indexed pages are returned. Without a keyword, it also does not represent normal rankings. To determine indexing of your own pages, prefer authorized Search Console URL Inspection; do not inspect another party's private property.

Separate competitor claims by source: page self-report / verifiable material cited by the page / researcher inference. Keep unobservable traffic, revenue, and outcome data unknown. The presence of a conversion button does not mean high conversion rates; long content does not mean high quality.
